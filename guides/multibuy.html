When “buy more, save more” costs more

A lower price per item and a lower grocery bill are different things. Look at both before you add extra items.

Calculate the checkout total

Multiply the normal item price by the required quantity, then subtract the discount. Compare that with the cost of buying only the quantity you planned to buy. Check whether taxes, shipping, membership requirements, or minimum purchase thresholds change the result.

Example: One item costs $6. A “buy three, save $5” offer costs $13 at checkout. The unit price falls from $6 to about $4.33, but a shopper who needed one item spends $7 more today.

Ask three questions

  1. Will I use every item before it expires?
  2. Can I afford the extra cash outlay this week?
  3. Is there another brand or package with a lower total cost?

Gift card promotions deserve a separate check: a future store credit is not the same as cash off today's purchase. It helps only if you would make another eligible purchase and use it before expiry.

Try your own numbers in the deal calculator. Its result is an estimate before tax; the retailer's terms and checkout price control the final total.
