Compare groceries by unit price

The shelf price tells you what you pay today. The unit price tells you how much food you get for each dollar.

Use the same unit

Divide the package price by its weight, volume, or count. Compare two products using the same measure: dollars per ounce, dollars per pound, or dollars per serving. Shelf labels often show unit prices, but check the unit because one tag may use ounces while another uses pounds.

Example: A 16-ounce package costs $4.00, or $0.25 per ounce. A 24-ounce package costs $5.40, or $0.225 per ounce. The larger package has the lower unit price, but costs $1.40 more at checkout.

Then check what you will use

The larger package only helps if you can use or safely store the extra amount before it goes bad. For foods with waste, a smaller pack can cost less overall. Also compare quality and ingredients when products are not equivalent.

A quick store checklist

  • Compare identical units.
  • Check the total cash due today.
  • Check package size and usable servings.
  • Account for storage space and spoilage.
  • Recheck the shelf or app price at your store.
